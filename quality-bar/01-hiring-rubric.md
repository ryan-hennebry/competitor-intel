# Hiring-manager rubric

The default rubric: what a hiring manager tests a work sample on, written to hold for any reader.
**Use it unmodified when the reader has no public writing, and say in `00-reader.md` that you did.**
Where the reader does write, the stage-1 agent swaps that reader's own quoted criteria into the rows
they bear on and keeps the rest of the table.

**Gate question:** would this person make the team better at the job the JD describes? It needs a
resounding yes, earned in the seconds page 1 gets: the reader has to see their own problem
understood before they read for who understood it. Judgement is the currency; effort is not.

| ID | Criterion | Test the red-team runs | Blocking |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **A tool with a user.** It answers a question the company actually has, conclusion first. | Does the first bold sentence state a decision-relevant judgement about a question the JD's topics imply? | Yes if no |
| H2 | **Nothing already known.** A fact the reader already holds about their own company is spent page. | Is the main clause of any claim-header, lede sentence or recommendation already in the reader's KNOWN table? | Yes |
| H3 | **Visible subtraction.** It reads as ruthlessly cut, not as everything found. | Would the reader miss each section and each table row? Name any whose deletion loses nothing. | Material |
| H4 | **A defensible, falsifiable claim.** A thesis a reader can disagree with beats a neutral survey. | Can the reader falsify the lede in ten seconds from a link on the page? Is the case against stated? | Yes if no |
| H5 | **Numbers where they exist, honest absence where they don't.** An invented number is worse than a missing one. | Any number or absence without its qualifier or bound? | Yes |
| H6 | **Primary evidence the reader can click, and the derivation behind every derived claim.** | Does each load-bearing claim link to a primary page? Does a derived claim, the distribution claim above all, carry the reasoning and the evidence it rests on, not only its conclusion? | Material |
| H7 | **Print craft.** Error-free and well set on the page it prints on. | Any typo, broken link, split table, widow, or label that does not match its contents? | Yes |
| H8 | **One deliberate moment of voice.** Restraint, plus one line that lands. | Quote the line that lands. Quote the three most machine-sounding sentences. | Material if none lands |

**Filed with the rest if:** it reads like a template applied to the company; a grid with no
argument; every claim hedged; one typo or broken link; visibly LLM-default in prose rhythm; or long
because length signals effort.

Everything below is one hiring manager's own wording, from Archy de Berker's "Be a shiny needle" as
a reader agent reported it; its URL is `[Unknown]`, so the phrasing is reported and not fetched.
Recorded here 2026-09-18 as calibration of how hard a page is graded — never as this reader's stated
view, and never a line of a brief. It is an instance of the rows above, not a row: where the run's
own reader writes, that reader's quoted criteria take its place.

- H1 — the brief answers its question "like the Amazon buy-now button".
- H2 — "Anything the brief tells you about your own company that you already knew is wasted page."
- H3 — the editing pass is "mostly going to consist of *removing* things".
- H5 — "Fabricated precision is the worse failure."
- H6 — the side project somebody can interact with.
- H7 — "A single sheet of error-free, decent-looking A4."
- H8 — "Dry, not zany".
- The gate — he "does not buy effort", and calls his own judging "capricious and vain".
